	\chapter*{Abstract}
	
	%Software forms the backbone of modern society, supporting critical infrastructure such as communication networks, financial systems, and healthcare services. The security of these systems is a pressing concern, as vulnerabilities can lead to severe consequences, including financial losses, reputational harm, and even threats to human safety.
	
	Emerging threats like advanced persistent attacks, ransomware, and supply chain vulnerabilities require more dynamic and scalable security solutions than traditional methods can offer. This term paper investigates the potential of \ac{AI}-driven penetration testing, specifically through the use of \ac{LLM}s to automate the process of penetration testing. Recent developments such as PentestGPT, PentestAgent, and ARTEMIS will be discussed.
	
	PentestGPT and PentestAgent are capable of performing automated penetration tests in controlled environments. Tools like XBOW are able to make valuable findings in bug bounty programs. However, proprietary or niche technology stacks clearly mark their limits. Security experts note that while \ac{AI}-driven tools excel at identifying and exploiting known vulnerabilities, they lack the lateral movement and contextual adaptability that human testers provide. Additionally, security risks such as data leakage through prompt injection and biases in training data pose further challenges.

	Future research should focus on enhancing \ac{LLM}s' ability to navigate proprietary systems, reducing false positives, and addressing security risks.
