\chapter{Penetration Testing}
\label{ch:chapter04}
"[P][...]enetration testing is a detailed procedure to unravel [...] open doors in a system or network." \cite{Lachkov_2022} The technique of penetration testing is a creative process of understanding IT-systems and their detailed structure and functionality. Finding and documenting vulnerabilities in IT-infrastructure helps to secure them and enhance the safety of these systems.

\ac{DAST} scanners are able to test software for vulnerabilities by replaying known attack patterns. In comparison to this, penetration tests are more creative approaches. A tester adopts his testing plans based on the reactions of the System Under Test and sets up his plans based on architectural details. Penetration tests in fact are able to simulate full blown realistic attacks against a System Under Test. Findings from these realistically simulated attacks reveal in detail how real world attackers could exploit existing vulnerabilities. These information are being documented in a test report to inform "IT infrastructure management" \cite{Lachkov_2022} and propose possible mitigation strategies.

If a corporate vendor decides to conduct a penetration test on its system this test can be performed either by its own employees or by a third party specializes company. Either way the goal of finding exploitable vulnerabilities needs to be specified in detail. You can refer to the example of stealing a car. By a penetration test a specialist can find out how hard it is to steal your car but this appears not to be quite specific. Stealing a car usually refers to someone driving another's car without permission and not returning it. Does a unknown driving one's car without permission refers to stealing it when it is returned next day? Does a teenager driving its parents car without parents permission refer to stealing it? The goal of finding out how hard it is to steal a car is simply not specific enough. For IT infrastructure a specific goal could for example be: Is a unauthorized party able to interfere with \ac{CIA} triad? \cite{Bishop_2007}

In the next step it is important to consider the knowledge the penetration tester has about the System Under Test. Potential attackers can allocate massive resources in form of time and money if the exploitation seams worthwhile. Through providing penetration testers with information about the System Under Test they can "compensate for the disparity in resources." \cite{Bishop_2007} Also penetration testers can be given partially internal access to simulate the scenario of already overcome first barriers by attackers. \cite{Bishop_2007} penetration testing with different degrees of knowledge can be categorized into Black-, Grey- and White-Box penetration testing. Black-Box penetration testing means that the penetration tester has no internal knowledge about the System Under Test. White-Box Testing means the Tester has complete knowledge of the internals and Grey-Box refers to partial knowledge of internal structures.  \cite{Rane_2024}
