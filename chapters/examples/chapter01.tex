\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}
Software is integral to wide-ranging aspects of daily life, underpinning communication technologies, payment services, and health systems. Failures, unavailability, or data leaks in these crucial services can lead to severe harm or even death. Consequently, society has a significant indirect interest in software security due to its heavy reliance on digital infrastructure. \cite{Wilson_2020}

Beyond societal concerns, software vendors also have compelling interests in providing secure software. On the one hand, companies can financially benefit from voluntarily disclosing their security measures. This transparency and proactive approach can positively influence their market value. \cite{Gordon_2010}

On the other hand, software vendors are mandated to comply with various data protection laws and regulations. Non-compliance can result in severe penalties, including fines of up to 20,000,000€ limited to 4\% of their total worldwide annual turnover. \cite{GDPR}

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:intro:problem_statement}
These needs of cyber security need to be satisfied while surrounded by a dynamically and fast evolving threat landscape. State and non-state actors drive a wide and diverse landscape of cyber security threats. Supply chain attacks, advanced persistent threats and vulnerabilities on the Internet of Things are just some cyber security threats modern systems face. \cite{Adeyeri_2024} Thus developing and maintaining software systems securely and protecting them from any harm through cyber criminals gets harder ever since.

\section{Goal of research}
\label{sec:intro:goal}
% todo: quellen mit 3 measures of software security alignen
To address these growing difficulties of securing software systems, corporate vendors and developers can apply several security measures.

The \ac{SDLC} describes multiple steps in which the development of secure software can be split up. One central part of the development of secure software is testing. \cite{McGraw_2004} A highly effective approach of software testing is Penetration Testing \cite{Lachkov_2022}. Penetration Testing used to be a manual and creative approach of dynamically testing for security vulnerabilities in software. However, traditional thus manual methods of Penetration Testing are no longer sufficient to keep track of fast evolving threat landscapes and ever growing software systems. This raises the need of automating and speeding up Penetration Testing. \cite{Stefinko_2024}

This term paper will discuss how \ac{AI}-driven systems can support penetration testers. It will also discuss to which degree Penetration Testing already can be automate and thus help Penetration Testers to focus of more advances research and testing.

\section{Research Question and Hypotheses}
\label{sec:intro:research_question}
The central research question addressed in this term paper is following: RQ: Are \ac{LLM}s capable of performing automated penetration tests in corporate environments?\\

\noindent The hypotheses to answer this question is following: H: \ac{LLM}s are capable of performing automated penetration tests on corporate and proprietary applications.
