\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:chapter06}

Corporate networks span hundreds or thousands of hosts. Large corporate networks let Reinforcement Learning approaches reach their limits. The training process of Reinforcement Learning approaches, like PenBox, requires high-fidelity training environments. \cite{Schwartz_2019} 
Automating penetration testing with \ac{AI} models trained through Reinforcement Learning has significant limitations. These approaches typically concentrate on exploiting known vulnerabilities through Metasploit and struggle with dynamic environments, making them unable to identify complex business logic vulnerabilities and zero-day exploits.

The vulnerabilities exploited by PentestGPT, PentestAgent, and ARTEMIS in benchmarks are nearly all based on known vulnerabilities or performed in controlled, unrealistic test environments. For corporate vendors, web application penetration testing is particularly interesting because web applications are highly exposed and widely targeted. Especially PentestAgent targets at exploiting known vulnerabilities instead of investigating architecture of web applications to find new vulnerabilities. For finding new vulnerabilities in unknown web applications these tools are not made and tested for.

PentestGPT was evaluated based on 13 \ac{CTF} challenges, and PentestAgent's was evaluated based on 11 \ac{CTF} challenges, which is even fewer. The selection process for these challenges is opaque. PentestGPT was evaluated on hand-picked challenges, while PentestAgent was evaluated on randomly selected ones. Both selecting processes can influence the evaluation result.

Corporate \ac{LLM} driven penetration testing tools like XBOW and oss-fuzz clearly show that \ac{LLM}s are capable of finding security vulnerabilities in corporate software.

When proprietary technology stacks, including niche frameworks, XBOW reaches its limits in investigating proprietary codebases. Security experts who tested XBOW concluded that it lacks the lateral movement capabilities required to thoroughly investigate how applications function under test. This limitation is also observable in ARTEMIS's evaluation.
