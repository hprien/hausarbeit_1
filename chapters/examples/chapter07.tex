\section{This is possible}
\label{sec:chapter06:possible}
Tools like BurpSuite, ZAP and SQLMap already implement partially automated penetration testing techniques through signature-driven \ac{DAST} scanning approaches. \ac{AI} models trained through Reinforcement Learning are used in these tools.

With the rise of \ac{LLM}s into the public more and more research was conducted on leveraging \ac{LLM}s for automated penetration testing. Research revealed that already the inherit knowledge of a \ac{LLM} is capable of performing penetration tests. penetration testing with these is as simple as asking a publicly available \ac{LLM} like Mistral, Kimi-k or GPT to assist. The user then has to execute what the \ac{LLM} suggests to do. For example executing a \ac{CLI} command or opening a browser window.

By providing the \ac{LLM} with tool usage capabilities this process can be automated further. Also the inherit knowledge of the \ac{LLM} can be extended with external sources like Vulnerability Databases. With this it is possible to build fully automated penetration testing agents that actually find vulnerabilities.


\section{Functional limitations}
\label{sec:chapter06:limitations}
\ac{LLM}s are capable of performing penetration tests. However, these tend to show high false positive rates which can only partially be mitigated. Especially noteworthy is that they are often benchmarked with popular deliberately vulnerable applications like DVWA, OWASP Juice Shop and OWASP WebGoat. The vulnerabilities in these applications are widely known and there fore well represented in \ac{LLM}s training data. It may therefore be that \ac{LLM}s perform well at finding vulnerabilities in these webapps.

When it comes to performing penetration tests in proprietary tech stacks or applications built with niche frameworks, automated penetration tests leveraging \ac{LLM}s reach their limits. Tests with proprietary tools like XBOW revealed that even highly sophisticated tools struggle with niche and proprietary tech stacks. In general they are capable of finding issues in real world bug bounty programs though, as tools like XBOW are placed at the top of Hackerone leaderboard. \cite{hackerone_leaderboard} \cite{xbow_hype}


\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:chapter06:future_work}
With an appropriate setup, \ac{LLM}s can conduct end-to-end penetration tests. However, their performance in identifying vulnerabilities remains limited when applied to proprietary or niche technology stacks. Future research should prioritize investigating the underlying causes of these limitations and developing strategies to enhance AI-driven testing in such environments. Additionally, further study is needed to address and mitigate the security challenges associated with these approaches.
